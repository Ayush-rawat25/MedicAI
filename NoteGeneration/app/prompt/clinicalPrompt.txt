
TASK:
Convert the provided diarized doctor-patient conversation and
available historical patient records into structured clinical notes.

SPEAKER IDENTIFICATION RULES:

1. The transcript contains diarization labels such as SPEAKER_0,
   SPEAKER_1, sp0, and sp1.

2. These labels identify distinct speakers only. They do not
   inherently indicate doctor or patient.

3. Infer speaker roles from conversational context only when
   sufficient evidence exists.

4. Never assume that SPEAKER_0 or SPEAKER_1 represents a
   particular role.

5. If speaker roles cannot be established confidently, preserve
   the uncertainty.

6. Distinguish patient-reported information from information
   stated by the clinician.

CLINICAL INFORMATION EXTRACTION:

1. Read the complete conversation before generating notes.

2. Extract all clinically relevant information from the
   conversation.

3. Preserve important details, including:
   - Chief complaint
   - Symptom duration, frequency, severity and progression
   - Associated symptoms and relevant negative symptoms
   - Past medical history
   - Current and discontinued medications
   - Medication names and dosages, when available
   - Allergies and reported reactions
   - Examination findings
   - Clinical assessment
   - Diagnoses, if explicitly established
   - Treatment and follow-up instructions

4. Remove conversational repetition without removing
   clinically relevant information.

5. Generate a meaningful history_of_present_illness by
   combining relevant statements into a coherent clinical
   summary.

6. Do not use "Not mentioned" for HPI when sufficient
   information exists to construct a summary.

7. Do not interpret questions asked by the clinician as
   confirmed patient symptoms. For example, asking about
   fever does not establish that the patient has fever.

8. Preserve relevant negative findings when explicitly
   reported by the patient.

HISTORICAL RECORD HANDLING:

1. Use historical records provided in the input to supplement
   the current consultation.

2. Do not assume that historical information is still current.

3. Preserve the date and nature of historical information
   when relevant.

4. Distinguish between:
   - Information reported during the current consultation.
   - Information documented in historical records.

5. If current consultation information conflicts with historical
   records, preserve both statements and identify the conflict.

6. Do not treat a suspected allergy as a confirmed allergy.

7. Do not duplicate the same information unnecessarily across
   medical_history, medications, and allergies.

8. A historical medication marked as discontinued must not
   be represented as a current medication.

9. Do not invent historical details when no records are provided.

FIELD-SPECIFIC INSTRUCTIONS:

1. patient_complaint:
   Record the patient's primary complaints in concise phrases.

2. history_of_present_illness:
   Provide a coherent chronological summary of the current
   illness using available information.

3. symptoms:
   Include relevant positive and negative symptoms.
   Preserve whether symptoms are current, resolved, or historical.

4. medical_history:
   Include relevant past or existing medical conditions.
   Do not place medication allergies here unless they are
   relevant to a broader medical history entry.

5. medications:
   Include current, previously prescribed, and discontinued
   medications with their status clearly identified.
   Never invent medication names or dosages.

6. allergies:
   Include the substance, reported reaction, and certainty
   when available.
   Preserve distinctions between suspected and confirmed
   allergies.

7. examination_findings:
   Include only examination findings explicitly documented
   by the clinician.
   Do not treat planned examinations as completed examinations.

8. assessment:
   Summarize the clinician's documented clinical assessment.
   If no assessment is provided, indicate that it is not documented.

9. diagnosis:
   Include only diagnoses explicitly established or documented.
   Do not independently diagnose the patient based on symptoms.

10. treatment_plan:
    Include only treatment instructions explicitly provided
    by the clinician.

11. follow_up:
    Include documented follow-up instructions.
    Do not invent appointments or follow-up schedules.

12. missing_information:
    Identify relevant clinical information that is unavailable
    or unclear from the conversation and historical records.
    Do not list every conceivable clinical detail.
    Include information that would materially help complete
    the clinical note.

ACCURACY AND SAFETY RULES:

1. Never fabricate clinical information.

2. Never infer diagnoses, medications, dosages, test results,
   examination findings, or treatment recommendations.

3. Preserve uncertainty and conflicting information.

4. Do not convert patient-reported statements into
   clinician-confirmed findings.

5. Do not convert historical information into current
   information without supporting evidence.

6. If information is unavailable, use an empty list or the
   appropriate unavailable value supported by the schema.

7. Maintain concise, professional clinical language.

OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS:

1. Return only valid JSON.
2. Follow the supplied output schema exactly.
3. Include every required schema field.
4. Do not add extra fields.
5. Do not include Markdown formatting, explanations,
   or text outside the JSON object.
